\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We evaluated whether a simple selectivity-aware routing rule can improve filtered vector search in PostgreSQL while
satisfying retrieval-quality constraints. The router estimates matching rows from PostgreSQL's cached histogram
statistics and chooses exact search below a calibrated threshold and iterative HNSW above it. On 2{,}700 range-filtered
queries over 100{,}000 LAION/CLIP vectors, the two strategies have opposite cost trends that cross between 2\% and 5\%
selectivity (RQ1). Calibrated on 70\% of the query vectors and evaluated once on the rest, the router achieved a mean
strict Recall@10 of 0.990 with complete results, a median latency of 2.50\,ms and a p95 of 5.33\,ms. The best fixed
strategies meeting the same targets reach 4.57\,ms and 26.28\,ms, and the router captures essentially all of the
headroom identified by a hindsight oracle (RQ2). PostgreSQL's default plan choice was faster but incomplete for 27\% of
queries. At individual levels the router is at best on par with the strategy it selects; it pays a tail penalty near
the crossover, where the better strategy depends on the query rather than on its row count, and is slightly slower
than single-pass HNSW at the broadest levels (RQ3). These findings hold for
one dataset, one attribute and one warm-cache, single-client environment. Future work should cover multi-attribute
and categorical predicates, larger collections, concurrent and cold-cache workloads, estimators aware of correlation
between filters and vector neighborhoods, and a planner cost model for iterative index scans that accounts for result
completeness.
