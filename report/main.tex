\begin{abstract}
In PostgreSQL with pgvector, a filtered top-$k$ vector query can be answered exactly by filtering and ranking every
eligible row, or approximately by an HNSW index scan that filters candidates after retrieval. Exact search slows down
as more rows match, while HNSW loses recall or returns too few rows under selective filters. We evaluate a lightweight
router that estimates the number of matching rows from PostgreSQL's own column statistics and runs exact search when
the estimate is at most a threshold $T$, and iterative HNSW otherwise. $T$ is calibrated on 70\% of the query vectors
and evaluated once on the rest. On 100{,}000 LAION/CLIP vectors and 2{,}700 range-filtered queries spanning
0.1\%--90\% selectivity, the router reaches a mean Recall@10 of 0.990 with complete results, a median latency of
2.50\,ms and a 95th-percentile latency of 5.33\,ms, against 4.57\,ms and 26.28\,ms for the best fixed strategies
meeting the same quality targets. PostgreSQL's default plan choice is faster but returns incomplete results for 27\%
of queries.
\end{abstract}
