\begin{abstract}
Vector search inside relational databases is usually combined with metadata predicates. In PostgreSQL with pgvector,
a filtered top-$k$ query can be answered exactly by filtering through a B-tree and ranking every eligible row, or
approximately by an HNSW index scan that filters candidates after retrieval. Exact search becomes slow as more rows
match, while HNSW loses recall or returns fewer than $k$ rows under selective filters. We evaluate a lightweight
router that estimates the number of matching rows from PostgreSQL's own column statistics and runs exact search when
the estimate is at most a threshold $T$, and HNSW with iterative scanning otherwise. $T$ is calibrated on 70\% of the
query vectors and evaluated once on the remaining 30\%. On 100{,}000 512-dimensional LAION/CLIP vectors and 2{,}700
range-filtered queries spanning 0.1\%--90\% selectivity, the router achieves a mean strict Recall@10 of 0.991 with
complete results, a median latency of 1.33\,ms and a 95th-percentile latency of 3.30\,ms. The best fixed strategies
meeting the same quality targets reach 2.72\,ms and 16.34\,ms. PostgreSQL's default plan choice is faster but returns
incomplete results for 27\% of queries. The router is not the fastest method at any single selectivity level; its
benefit comes from avoiding each fixed strategy's worst regime.
\end{abstract}
